\problem{Convergence of Newton's root finding method}
    In the lecture we have stated:

    \bluebox{If $g$ is continuously differentiable in a neighborhood
around a root $x^\star$ with nonzero derivative at $x^\star$, 
then there exists a neighborhood around $x^\star$ where Newton's 
iteration converges to $x^\star$ for all starting values.}

    Now consider the function

    \begin{equation}
        g(x) = x(1+x^2)^{-\frac{1}{2}}
    \end{equation}

    \begin{enumerate}
        \item What is the root $x^\star$ of $g$?
        \item Calculate $g'(x^\star)$. What does this imply for Newton iteration?
        \item Explicitly calculate the newton update step
        \begin{equation}
            x_{k+1} = x_{k} - \frac{g(x_k)}{g'(x_k)}
        \end{equation}
        \item Consider the following cases for the initialization of 
        the Newton iteration
        \begin{itemize}
            \item $x_0 = 0.5$
            \item $x_0 = -1.0$
            \item $x_0 = 2.0$
        \end{itemize}
        and analyze if the iteration converges, diverges or oscillates.
    \end{enumerate}
